What is in each issue

Evidence you can put in a placement note

Built for people who have to explain a corridor to a client, an underwriter or a trading desk this week.

01
Corridor status
Normal, strained, disrupted or severe for Hormuz, Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb, Suez and the Cape, set by a published rule. Any analyst override is printed with its reason.
02
Radar vessel presence
Our own count of vessels in the Strait of Hormuz from free Copernicus Sentinel-1 radar imagery, compared with the 2025 baseline as a 30-day average. Radar sees ships whether or not their AIS is on.
03
Warning-indicator checklists
For each corridor, a fixed list of indicators (attacks reported, seizures, conflict events, listings, radar presence) with what is lit this week and the dated source behind each.
04
Incidents and context
Counts of incidents reported in the trade press, conflict-event signals built on open GDELT and UCDP data, and individual third-party figures quoted from public statements with their source.
05
Cost pressure
Energy and commodity benchmarks from the U.S. EIA and the World Bank, and the New York Fed's Global Supply Chain Pressure Index.
06
Method and sources
Every number carries its date and source. Sources are open or licensed for commercial use, and the method is printed in each issue.

What the radar can and cannot tell you. The Hormuz count measures vessel presence, not transits or direction, and cannot identify a ship. Anchored, waiting and naval vessels are counted, so the figure never falls to zero. We publish 30-day averages only. The report shows observations and indicators; it is not a forecast of events, prices or rates, and not investment, insurance or legal advice.
