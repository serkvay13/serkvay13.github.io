THIS WEEK'S BRIEFING
US-Iran Conflict, American Public Opinion & the 2026 Midterm Election Outlook
NERAI assesses the US-Iran conflict as a net electoral liability for President Trump and congressional Republicans: no rally effect has materialised, energy costs are feeding economic dissatisfaction, and independents are breaking against the incumbent. Base case: Democrats recapture the House (62%); Republicans narrowly hold the Senate (58%).

No rally, rising costs: unlike 1991 (Bush ~89%) or post-9/11 (Bush ~86%), Trump has seen no durable approval bounce; Brent +~12%/30d to ~$89 and Hormuz -73% (SEVERE) turn conflict into a pocketbook issue for swing-state voters.

House base case - Democratic recapture (62%): a ~10-seat Republican net loss (range -6 to -18), below the historical ~28-seat midterm penalty thanks to a redistricting buffer, but enough to flip control; decisive in ~3-5 suburban toss-ups.

Senate base case - Republicans hold (58%): on a corrected 2026 map, the competitive seats are Democratic-held Georgia (Ossoff) and open Michigan/New Hampshire, and Republican-held Maine, open North Carolina and the Ohio special - Montana, Ohio and West Virginia are Republican-held, and Arizona/Pennsylvania are not on the 2026 Senate ballot.

The tail risk: a single high-casualty escalation shock (aircraft downed, Hormuz mining, Iranian strike on Saudi oil) in the final 90 days could flip the dynamic - NERAI's Iran Military Escalation index is RISING; the 31 July Damietta drone strike was unclaimed and is not attributed to Iran.

THEME 1
Strait of Hormuz and Middle East Energy Security

ADNOC reported 15 vessel attacks in the region, underscoring a significant escalation in maritime security incidents near the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran is assessed to be potentially preparing measures that could permanently block the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global oil trade passes.

Despite deepening Middle East disruptions, oil traders remain broadly bearish on prices, suggesting markets are not yet fully pricing in a supply shock scenario.

Sources: ADNOC Reports 15 Vessel Attacks as Hormuz Risks Mount · Is Iran Preparing to Permanently Block the Strait of Hormuz?
THEME 2
Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey Trilateral Defense Pact

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey formally agreed to a mutual defense pact, representing one of the most significant non-NATO security alignments to emerge in the Muslim world in recent years.

Analysts framed the pact as a response to perceived gaps in US security guarantees and a broader Middle Eastern drive to build NATO-style collective defense architectures.

Pakistan's nuclear capability, Turkey's advanced drone and defense industrial base, and Saudi Arabia's financial resources are seen as complementary pillars of the new arrangement.

Sources: Why Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey just signed a defense · The Middle East Tries Its Hand at a NATO-Style Pact
THEME 3
Russia Sanctions, Ukraine War and Post-Putin Scenarios

The US Senate passed legislation granting the executive branch a tariff weapon specifically targeting Russia, though uncertainty remains over whether the Trump administration will deploy it.

Experts emphasized that correct implementation and enforcement of the Russia sanctions bill would be critical to its effectiveness, warning that poorly enforced measures have historically been circumvented.

Ukraine's air defense posture is deteriorating as Patriot missile supplies dwindle, creating a growing vulnerability to Russian air strikes.
