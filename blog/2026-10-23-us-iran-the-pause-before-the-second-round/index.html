The calls

The war has paused, not ended. NERAI expects the United States and Israel to open a second, short and intense strike round on Iran after the 3 November US vote, most likely in the second half of November. Iran will answer sideways, against Gulf energy, US bases and shipping, rather than in kind. By the new year the conflict most likely settles back into blockade, a tanker war and talks through Qatar and Pakistan.
Our pair models read the last 30 days between the US and Iran as words without matching action: threats and force are both below the pair's own wartime year. That is the pre-election lull the White House promised, not a de-escalation. Force posture, dated war plans and the sea campaign around Hormuz point the other way. The strongest model signal is economic: new sanctions designations against Iran are high for the coming month, and Iran's interstate military escalation reading is high.

Where things stand on 23 October

- **The war.** The US and Israel opened the war on 28 February 2026. A ceasefire on 8 April led to the Islamabad memorandum, signed on 17 June; it collapsed on 8 July after Iranian attacks on ships in Hormuz. The US reinstated its naval blockade of Iranian ports, and strikes resumed in early September [1][2]. - **The pause.** On 8 October President Trump said there would be no strikes before the 3 November midterms [3][10]; the White House repeated it on 22 October: "The United States will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections" [3][4]. - **The plans.** US and Israeli militaries have drawn up plans for "a short but intensive operation" after the midterms, with energy infrastructure, nuclear sites including Pickaxe Mountain, and missile and drone arsenals as likely targets; jets have rehearsed over Iraq and Defence Secretary Hegseth met Prime Minister Netanyahu in Israel this week [4][5]. A person briefed on the planning told NPR that Israel wanted to strike in mid-October and that Trump objected [6].
- **The sea.** October is the worst month of the war for shipping: 27 attacks on merchant ships to 22 October, according to the IMO, against 22 in March [7]. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said seven tankers were hit on 21–22 October; Washington called the claims fabricated [8]. - **The threats.** Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Iran would hit oil facilities, undersea cables, data centres and AI sites in up to seven Gulf countries if attacked again; the IRGC announced an "offensive" doctrine [9][10]. - **The squeeze.** The rial has passed 2.6 million to the dollar on the open market and the central bank is selling reserves to defend it [11]. CENTCOM says the blockade has kept $25 billion of crude from export [1]. - **The talks.** Indirect contacts continue through Qatar and Pakistan; Iran's seven-day plan (reopen Hormuz against an end to the blockade, sanctions relief and frozen assets) stays on the table [10][9].

What NERAI's own record shows: the lull is real. Military events between the two in October run well below their wartime average, yet still several times the 2025 level. NERAI's pair reading for the last 30 days is "rhetorical tension without matching action". We read it as a pause decided in Washington, not a turn toward peace.

2 · Will they strike, who first, what and how

Verdict: yes. A second US–Israeli strike round on Iran before 23 April 2027 is NERAI's base case, most likely in November 2026.

  • Who first. A joint opening, US-led. Israeli sources told ABC News the decision is "only for Trump to decide" and that even a unilateral Israeli strike would need US consent [4]. Israel will not move first on its own before its government is formed; Netanyahu would prefer Iran to fire first [9].
  • What. Energy and petrochemical export infrastructure (to squeeze the economy and force concessions), deeply buried nuclear sites including Pickaxe Mountain and the Isfahan tunnels, missile and drone production and launchers, IRGC naval assets [5][4].
  • How. A short, intense campaign of a few days: carrier and land-based aviation, standoff missiles, cyber operations, with the blockade kept in place. The US is concentrating three carriers, an extra amphibious group and roughly 9,000 additional sailors and Marines in the region [1][9][13].
  • What it is not. Not a regime-change ground war. Netanyahu has pledged to topple Iran's government [10]; Washington's is a better deal. The gap between the two decides how long the round lasts.

3 · Ground forces: boots on decks, not on the ground

  • Certain and ongoing: maritime seizures. Marines of the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit have boarded tankers trying to run the blockade; US forces have redirected and disabled blockade-runners [7][18].
  • Tail risk: raids. Options under discussion since March include a special-forces operation against highly enriched uranium buried at Isfahan and a seizure of Kharg Island, Iran's main export terminal [19][20]. Kharg has been struck, not seized [18]. Any raid would mean US troops holding ground under fire, which explains the reluctance [20].
  • Not on the table: invasion. No pre-positioning consistent with a ground campaign has been reported; the build-up is air, naval and amphibious.
  • Watch for: airborne units moving into Gulf staging bases, special-operations aircraft surges at Gulf bases, and Iranian claims of captured personnel.

6 · The chokepoints

**Strait of Hormuz: SEVERE (NERAI status, floor set on 20 October).** The Joint Maritime Information Center rates the strait SEVERE and the Arabian Gulf SUBSTANTIAL [33]. Lloyd's List counted 104 non-Iran-linked transits in the week to 11 October [7]. War-risk cover for a transit costs roughly thirty times its pre-war rate [35]. During a strike round, expect the most intense week of attacks and mining claims of the war, and ship-to-ship transfer zones and anchorages to be hit. **Bab el-Mandeb and Red Sea: DISRUPTED.** The Houthi blockade on Saudi-linked shipping has been in force since 20 July; Saudi-backed forces retook ground around the strait in early October [33][36]. Suez traffic was still well below pre-2023 levels in September [37], yet container lines are returning fast; a new round would stop that return first [38]. **Energy.** Brent spot was $115 on 20 October, against $71 on the eve of the war and a $138 peak on 7 April [39]. A strike round pushes prices back toward the April peak zone for days; the speed of the relief depends on whether Gulf export sites are hit.

7 · The escalation ladder

0 · Today: blockade and tanker war. US blockade of Iranian ports; IRGC attacks and mining claims in and around Hormuz; Houthi–Saudi war; talks via Qatar and Pakistan. Tripwire: a mass-casualty hit on US forces or a sinking with heavy loss of life.
1 · Short strike round (NERAI: November). A few days of US–Israeli strikes on energy, buried nuclear and missile targets. Analogs: 22 June 2025 strikes on Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan; the September 2026 strike cycle. Off-ramp: a declared end of operations plus an offer through Doha.
2 · Horizontal retaliation (likely, days 1–5). Iranian fire on Israel, US bases and Gulf energy and digital sites. Analogs: April and October 2024 salvos on Israel; the March 2026 strike on Ras Laffan. Off-ramp: US restraint on follow-on strikes; Gulf pressure on both sides.
3 · Two-strait closure attempt (plausible). Full Hormuz closure attempt with mines and swarms; Houthis close the Red Sea to Western-linked shipping. Analog: the 2024 Red Sea campaign at Hormuz scale. Off-ramp: the Hormuz-for-blockade swap.
4 · Nuclear threshold (tail). NPT withdrawal notice or evidence of weaponisation work; a US raid on uranium sites or regime-targeting strikes. Analog: none in this war; January 2020 (Soleimani) shows how a decapitation strike can end in a telegraphed, contained reply. Off-ramp: uranium exported to a third country under IAEA supervision.

What it means for decision-makers

  • Shipping and energy. Plan for a strike week in the second half of November: hold Gulf-bound cargoes outside the Gulf of Oman during that window, expect war-risk cover to be repriced at short notice, and assume the Red Sea return pauses.
  • People and sites. The US has ordered non-emergency staff out of Saudi Arabia [10]; Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Bahrain host the highest-value targets. Review shelter and evacuation plans for staff near energy, port and data-centre sites.
  • Compliance. NERAI's sanctions reading for Iran is high for the coming month: screen counterparties, ship-to-ship transfer partners and payment routes.
  • Markets. Price a short spike, not a regime change: NERAI's base case returns to blockade and talks by January.
